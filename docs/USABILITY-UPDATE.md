# Map and rover usability update

The seven top-level workspaces now display separately, retaining their DOM and mission state when switching. Route Planner has separate Map & waypoints, Compare routes, Altitude & slope, Science sources and Mission settings views.

Ares Experience has prominent Add rover & drive, Walk Mars and Guided expedition buttons. Auto-follow begins movement immediately; users can disable it and use W/A/S/D or the visible directional controls. Repeated route refreshes with unchanged geometry no longer reset an active run. Actual geometry changes still require a restart.

Wheel/trackpad scroll over the local map zooms toward the pointer with bounded map coverage. Scroll and browser pinch events over the globe change its zoom; explicit plus/minus buttons remain available.

Thermal and chemistry overlays have prominent controls beside the map. Thermal is the bundled historical THEMIS display mosaic; brightness is not calibrated temperature. Chemistry uses the actual NASA georeferenced CRISM MTRDR SET mosaic at https://data.nasa.gov/docs/datasets/public/CRISM-Mosaic/jezero_crater_mosaic_SET_OPT_TAN_rect_flightCTX.tif . Its Mars equirectangular projection uses a sphere of radius3396190m and nominal18m source pixels. It is resampled to512×512 across the local footprint, ~94% valid display coverage. These RGB spectral variations are not concentration measurements, current reactions or a chemical hazard forecast. No single acquisition time is supplied by this multiscene GeoTIFF.

Radiation opens the Gale Crater region and shows the historical Curiosity RAD summary with its source, place and observation window. The region centre is geographic context, not a precise RAD sample position. No Jezero dose raster is available and no radiation heatmap is invented.

Fifty-five USGS mapped Aeb aeolian bedform polygons are transformed from spherical Transverse Mercator (central meridian77.5E, radius3396190m) and rasterized with all_touched=true on the existing256×256 DEM grid. They are interpreted as unconsolidated sediment; default avoidance is a selectable prototype policy, not an official NASA operational no-go designation. Source: Sun and Stack2020 SIM3464, hosted FeatureServer/8 at https://services.arcgis.com/v01gqwM5QqNysAAi/arcgis/rest/services/Jezero_FeatureServiceFromPro/FeatureServer/8 . Geometry was generalized by the source query with10m maximum offset. Purple shows these mapped deposits; red shows modeled slope/missing-data exclusions. Small obstacles, current terrain conditions and actual traversability remain unverified.

Validation:23 automated tests pass, including actual mapped-deposit routing avoidance, disabling that policy while preserving slope limits, existing ownership isolation, temporal filtering and energy units. Browser checks verified section navigation, route comparison, thermal and CRISM controls, both local-map and globe wheel zoom, radiation-region navigation, rover auto-follow and manual W movement, pause, and excursion state preserved while switching sections. No physical trackpad device was used; browser wheel interaction exercised the same handler. Existing historical source views and saved mission functions are retained.
